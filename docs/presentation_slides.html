Handoff from Preprocessing (Part 1)

The preprocessing pipeline turned 400 raw EEG samples into clean temporal power envelopes:

  • Input shape per trial: (36, 15) → 36 spectral-spatial channels × 15 time steps (0.5 s windows).
  • Spatial & spectral work is already done: 9 sub-bands (4–40 Hz) × 4 CSP components.
  • HWNet's goal: Model only the temporal evolution (15 time steps) without overfitting.

1. Small Temporal Kernels (1×3)

A kernel length of 3 windows covers ~1.0 s of motor imagery—sufficient to track ERD onset without ballooning weights.

2. Aggressive Regularization

Dual 50% Dropout + Batch Normalization + max_norm(0.5) constraint on weights to strictly prevent overfitting.

Architecture Comparison: HWNet vs. MixNet

Why we deliberately chose a simple, compact classifier over a complex autoencoder.

Dimension MixNet (Baseline) HWNet (Ours) Why HWNet is better suited
Input Domain Raw time series: (1, 400, 18) Windowed power: (1, 15, 36) Lower dimensional, higher signal-to-noise ratio
Trainable Parameters 44,777 parameters 5,362 parameters ~8× smaller; avoids memorizing EEG noise
Objective / Loss Reconstruction (MSE) + Triplet + CE Single-task Cross-Entropy No loss balancing or triplet margin tuning
Receptive Field Kernels: 64 and 32 samples Kernels: 3 windows (~1.0 s) Matched directly to windowed ERD latency
Training Time (9 subs) ~18 minutes (GPU) ~4.5 minutes (GPU) 4× faster iteration & checkpointing

Core Rationale: When preprocessing already extracts strong spatial and spectral filters, an end-to-end autoencoder with 40k+ weights is unnecessary. A clean 5k parameter CNN generalizes better across sessions.

Per-Subject Analysis & Key Takeaways

Subject-level breakdown reveals why windowed temporal features succeeded.

Where HWNet Wins

  • Subject 5: 76.81% vs. 60.83% (+15.98%)
    MixNet's global average smoothed out S05's short-lived ERD burst. HWNet's 0.5 s windows isolated it cleanly.
  • Subject 8: 93.47% vs. 90.42% (+3.05%)
    Highest performer in the cohort. Strong bilateral separation.
  • Subject 3: 92.22% vs. 91.94%
    Consistent ceiling-level performance across sessions.

Neurophysiology: The S02 Case

  • Subject 2: 49.44% (near chance)
    A classic case of BCI Inefficiency (present in 15–30% of users). The subject lacks distinguishable sensorimotor $\mu$-rhythm suppression. Neither model can extract signal from noise.
  • Excluding S02: HWNet's cohort mean reaches 80.59%.

Conclusion: Combining domain-specific FBCSP windowing with a compact temporal CNN (5k params) beats an overparameterized 40k-parameter multi-task autoencoder on small EEG datasets.
